The method

How the analysis works

1

Find your peers

We look up how athletes (or teams) in your division who finish in about your time typically split their race: every run, every station and Roxzone. The comparison slides with your exact time, so a 61-minute and an 89-minute racer are never measured against the same benchmark.

2

Compare every split

Each split is set against that typical split, in seconds, and against how spread out your peers are. Ratings follow where you sit among them: the middle half is the "normal range"; the slowest quarter is "time to gain" (the slowest tenth a "big opportunity"); the fastest quarter is a "strength" (the fastest tenth a "big strength").

3

Rank the time

Your biggest gaps are ranked in seconds, and your goal plan shows where the time to your next milestone usually comes from, using how athletes at that goal split their race.

Where the benchmarks come from

Koda built the benchmarks mostly from published summary statistics of recent HYROX results; the page itself holds no individual racer's results. Singles: 2025/26 split medians by finish time, covering about 325,000 finishes. Doubles: team split averages by finish time from Hyranking (which pools seasons without stating them), checked against medians from about 685,000 team finishes (2024/25 to 2026/27); a few doubles values at the fastest and slowest finish times were adjusted using team results listed on RoxTimes event pages. For each division we keep the typical time on each run, station and Roxzone across most of its field (from about 50–60 minutes up to 2–2½ hours, depending on the division), and how much racers who finish in the same time differ on each part (for doubles, estimated from singles racers at the same place in their field). Weights, distances and rules follow the official HYROX rulebooks.

  • HYROX: rulebooks for the 2025/26 and 2026/27 seasons (weights, distances, doubles rules).
  • athlete.tools: 2025/26 singles splits by finish time, how much same-finish racers differ, and finish-time percentiles.
  • rox-pace (MIT licence): finish-time percentiles for every division and doubles lap-by-lap medians, 2024/25 to 2026/27.
  • Hyranking: doubles splits by finish time (seasons pooled, not stated).
  • RoxTimes: doubles medians and event results, used to check and adjust the doubles tables and to split running from Roxzone (CC BY-NC 4.0, adapted by Koda).
  • Next Five: next-race gains by level from repeat racers, used for the suggested goal.
  • Research: Rappelt et al., Frontiers in Physiology (2026); Yamanoi, Winning Strategies in HYROX (2026, preprint).

Benchmarks last updated November 2026. They are Koda's estimates, not official HYROX figures, and Koda plans to refresh them each season. Koda is an official HYROX affiliate gym, but this analyzer is Koda's own tool: it isn't made, endorsed or checked by HYROX.

Good to know
  • Venues differ. Sled floors, run laps and timing-mat placement usually move a single split by 20–60 seconds, but at some venues (for example Berlin and London ExCeL) several minutes shift between running and Roxzone or stations, and some venues run Run 8 a minute or more long. Most US venues also log a slightly bigger share of the race as running. If your running looks slow and your Roxzone or stations fast (or the reverse), judge them together. Sleds and Roxzone get extra leeway in the ratings for this reason.
  • Run 1 isn't compared. The start lap is shorter or longer at many venues, so running is judged on Runs 2–⁠8 and you're matched with racers whose other splits add up like yours.
  • Altitude. The benchmarks come mostly from races near sea level. Racing at around 1,600 m (5,300 ft), as at HYROX Denver, usually costs a little time on the runs and ergs and barely touches the strength stations.
  • Doubles. Your splits are the team's, so the analysis is about the team, not each partner. Partners must run every kilometre together. Team-to-team spreads aren't published, so for doubles the "faster/slower than X% of teams" figures and the ratings are Koda's estimates borrowed from singles racers at the same place in their field, and the km-by-km typical line is the average doubles lap pattern adjusted for finish time.
  • Sims. A gym simulation uses different sleds, floors and run loops and doesn't time Roxzone the same way, so treat sled, run and Roxzone comparisons from a sim as a rough guide.
  • Penalties. Official times include penalties; splits don't. If your official time is more than about 15 seconds above what your splits add up to, a penalty is the usual reason (most are 15 seconds to 2 minutes; a missed run lap costs 3–7 minutes), and the goal plan shows it as its own line. If you leave Roxzone blank, it's worked out from your overall time, so any penalty is counted as Roxzone.
  • It's a comparison, not a verdict. "Typical" is how athletes who finish where you do usually split their race (it adds up to your finish), so it can sit a little off the exact middle of each split. One race is a snapshot, and all ages are pooled.
